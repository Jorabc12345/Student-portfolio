# Academic Content Blueprint

Phase 2 – Stage 4: Academic Structure Validation and Finalization

This is the consolidated academic reference for Level 1. It combines the Phase 2 inventory, folder mapping, reflection mapping, PLO mapping, and global-section mapping. Stable folder IDs are used for content references; names are configurable display data. The MED 102 grouping remains proposed and is not client-approved.

## 1. Authority and Status

The original **BukSU-COM Portfolio Instructions.pdf** is authoritative for academic wording, questions, evidence, confidentiality, and academic integrity. Client decisions are authoritative for the individual portfolio scope, Level 1 subject set, four-folder count, access model, and future-level objective.

- **Confirmed:** directly stated in the college document or client baseline.
- **Provisional:** a safe organizational alignment using source periods and neutral folder slots.
- **Pending client confirmation:** not approved in the supplied materials.

The roadmap and SRS may guide project organization and technical direction, but neither overrides the college document's academic wording.

## 3. Subject Content

### 3.1 MED 101 – Integrated Basic Sciences

The four source bimonthly periods are the four main folders. Each folder contains CERAE, Reflections, and Artifacts. The supplied names are temporary configurable display values.

| Folder ID / display value | Period | Content and evidence |
|---|---|---|
| MED101-F01 / 1st Bimonthly – Medical Foundations | 1st Bimonthly | Expectations; ease and difficulty; personal milestone with artifacts; improvement plan with artifacts |
| MED101-F02 / 2nd Bimonthly – Knowledge Development | 2nd Bimonthly | Comparison with the first period; ease and difficulty; personal milestone with artifacts; improvement plan with artifacts |
| MED101-F03 / 3rd Bimonthly – Learning Integration | 3rd Bimonthly | Semester realizations; ease and difficulty; personal milestone with artifacts; plan for fourth-bimonthly goals |
| MED101-F04 / 4th Bimonthly – Year-End Reflection | 4th Bimonthly | Achieved goals and difficulty; personal milestone with artifacts; year-end change |

The fourth period contains three questions in the source. No fourth question is added.

**Internal folder pattern:** `CERAE` · `Reflections` · `Artifacts`.

#### Exact MED 101 questions

**1st Bimonthly**

1. What were your personal expectations upon entering medical school?
2. What did you find easy? What did you find difficult?
3. What was a personal achievement you have attained and see as a personal milestone in this bimonthly? (Provide artifacts)
4. What do you intend to do to improve your grades/study habits/time management/group dynamics, etc.? (Provide artifacts)

**2nd Bimonthly**

1. How different was the 2nd Bimonthly to the 1st Bimonthly?
2. What did you find easy? What did you find difficult?
3. What was a personal achievement you have attained and see as a personal milestone in this bimonthly? (Provide artifacts)
4. What do you intend to do to improve your grades/study habits/time management/group dynamics, etc.? (Provide artifacts)

**3rd Bimonthly**

1. What are your realizations now that a semester has passed?
2. What did you find easy? What did you find difficult?
3. What was a personal achievement you have attained and see as a personal milestone in this bimonthly? (Provide artifacts)
4. What do you intend to do to fully realize your goals in the 4th Bimonthly?

**4th Bimonthly**

1. What were the personal goals that you’ve achieved? What did you find easy and difficult to achieve?
2. What was a personal achievement you have attained and see as a personal milestone in this bimonthly? (Provide artifacts)
3. What significant change have you noted at the end of one whole school year?

### 3.2 MED 102 – Family, Community, & Preventive Medicine I

Four main folder IDs and temporary display values are supplied, but the MED 102 grouping is not approved. All eight domains are preserved as separate sections inside their proposed parent folders and must not be omitted.

Domains retain their own identities. Relevant evidence may be cross-referenced across multiple domains without duplicating or merging the domain sections. For example, `Community_Health_Assessment.pdf` may link to Social determinants of health, Community engagement and social accountability, and Patient-centered care.

| Domain | Source-specified evidence | Proposed parent folder |
|---|---|---|
| Patient-centered care | Patient case analysis identifying biological, psychological, and social factors | MED102-F01 – Patient & Family-Centered Care (proposed) |
| Family-focused care | Genogram or family assessment with reflection | MED102-F01 – Patient & Family-Centered Care (proposed) |
| Social determinants of health | Social determinants analysis of a patient or community case | MED102-F02 – Health & Social Determinants (proposed) |
| Global and national health frameworks | SDG mapping worksheet linking health priorities with Philippine health programs | MED102-F02 – Health & Social Determinants (proposed) |
| Primary health care and Universal Health Care | Reflection and output from the mock Rural Health Unit activity | MED102-F03 – Healthcare Systems & Primary Care (proposed) |
| Systems thinking | Map or causal loop diagram with written analysis | MED102-F03 – Healthcare Systems & Primary Care (proposed) |
| Community engagement and social accountability | Community health needs analysis or community-oriented intervention proposal | MED102-F04 – Community & Professional Development (proposed) |
| Professional identity formation | Integrative reflection on learning and growth throughout MED 102 | MED102-F04 – Community & Professional Development (proposed) |

Every domain uses these four exact questions:

1. **What did I learn?** Identify the most important insight from the activity.
2. **What changed in my understanding?** Explain how the experience challenged or strengthened your previous beliefs about health, illness, or healthcare delivery.
3. **Why does this matter to the people and communities we serve?** Relate your learning to patient, family, or community needs, preferably in the context of Bukidnon.
4. **How will I apply this as a future physician?** Identify a specific action or change in your future approach.

### 3.3 MED 103 – Medical Humanities I

The four official themed periods are aligned to four provisional folder slots. The official themes and questions remain unchanged.

| Folder slot | Official period theme |
|---|---|
| 1 | Learning Identity, Historical Perspectives, and Humanistic Foundations |
| 2 | Patient-Centered Relationships, Advocacy, and Cultural Understanding |
| 3 | Professional Identity, Ethics, Accountability, and Well-Being |
| 4 | Ethical Leadership, Disaster Preparedness, Social Responsibility, and Year-End Integration |

The mapped content includes the following source-derived requirements:

- Period 1: learning identity, historical perspectives, patient-doctor relationship, empathy, respect, trust, professional responsibility, milestone, difficulty, artifact, and improvement action.
- Period 2: patient narratives or role-plays, listening, empathy, nonjudgmental communication, trust, advocacy, empowerment, autonomy, cultural integration, diversity, planetary/environmental health, Bukidnon context, cultural humility, and a practice plan.
- Period 3: professionalism, integrity, respect, responsibility, ethical dilemmas, compassion, honesty, confidentiality, accountability, patient history-taking, communication adaptation, teamwork, bias, cultural considerations, feedback, weaknesses, and self-care.
- Period 4: group work, leadership, management, ethical leadership, collaboration, quality improvement, patient safety, disaster preparedness, coordinated response, risk communication, recovery, vulnerable communities, service, social accountability, BukSU-COM values, year-end growth, and Level II commitments.

#### Exact MED 103 questions

**1st Bimonthly – Learning Identity, Historical Perspectives, and Humanistic Foundations**

1. How have the principles of andragogy, learning, and memory influenced the way you study and take responsibility for your learning as a medical student? Reflect on one study practice you changed or need to change, and explain how this relates to your “My Learning Philosophy” and “My Why.”
2. Which historical development or medical tradition—from early medicine through Philippine medicine—most challenged or enriched your understanding of healing and the role of the physician? How did it influence your respect for scientific evidence, patients’ beliefs, and the social context of care?
3. As you were introduced to the patient–doctor relationship, what personal assumption about being a “good doctor” did you begin to question? Using a class discussion, scenario, or observed interaction, explain how empathy, respect, trust, and professional responsibility may influence a patient’s experience.
4. What personal milestone and what difficulty best describe your development during this bimonthly? Identify an artifact that demonstrates your learning and formulate one specific, realistic action to improve your self-directed learning, reflection, or humanistic attitudes in the next bimonthly.

**2nd Bimonthly – Patient-Centered Relationships, Advocacy, and Cultural Understanding**

1. Recall a patient narrative, role-play, or learning scenario in which a patient’s feelings, beliefs, circumstances, or expectations differed from the physician’s assumptions. How did listening, empathy, nonjudgmental communication, and trust-building change your interpretation of the patient’s behavior? What would you say or do differently now?
2. When a patient has difficulty understanding, accepting, or carrying out a proposed care plan, how can a physician advocate for and empower the patient without undermining autonomy? Reflect on an example and explain how patient feedback could be used to improve communication, quality of care, or the patient’s experience.
3. How have the discussions on cultural integration, diversity, and planetary/environmental health changed the way you understand illness and access to care? Consider local Bukidnon communities, including Indigenous Peoples where relevant, and examine how culture, living conditions, environment, or personal bias may affect respectful and equitable clinical decisions.
4. What learning activity or artifact most clearly demonstrates your progress in patient-centered communication, cultural humility, or advocacy? Describe what was challenging, the feedback or realization that mattered most, and a specific plan for practicing these competencies more consistently.

**3rd Bimonthly – Professional Identity, Ethics, Accountability, and Well-Being**

1. How has your understanding of medical professionalism changed from viewing it as proper conduct to seeing it as a continuing commitment to patients, colleagues, and society? Reflect on a concrete situation in which your attitudes, integrity, respect, or sense of responsibility were tested.
2. Reflect on a simulated or observed professional dilemma involving compassion, honesty, confidentiality, accountability, a professional lapse, or peer behavior. Which ethical or professional principles—including those discussed in relation to the Hippocratic Oath and PMA/PRC standards—guided your judgment? What would you do, and why?
3. What did patient history-taking experiences or exercises in Pediatrics, OB-GYN, Internal Medicine, or Surgery teach you about adapting communication to different patients and working respectfully with health care teams? Identify a communication challenge, possible bias, or cultural consideration and explain how you would respond more effectively.
4. What evidence best demonstrates your growth in professionalism, teamwork, and self-awareness during this bimonthly? Explain how you will respond to feedback, address an identified weakness, and practice healthy self-care while maintaining professional responsibilities.

**4th Bimonthly – Ethical Leadership, Disaster Preparedness, Social Responsibility, and Year-End Integration**

1. What did your experiences in group work, leadership activities, or management discussions reveal about your strengths and limitations as a future member or leader of a health care team? How would you use ethical leadership, collaboration, and quality-improvement principles to promote patient safety and resolve a team challenge?
2. Reflect on a disaster-risk-reduction activity, simulation, or scenario relevant to a Philippine community. What did it teach you about preparedness, coordinated response, risk communication, and recovery? Explain how you would balance immediate patient needs, limited resources, and care for vulnerable community members.
3. How did your Student Social Responsibility Project or other community-oriented learning experience deepen your understanding of service, social accountability, and the core values of BukSU-COM? Identify one result or limitation, whose perspective you may have overlooked, and a specific improvement you would propose. (Provide an artifact.)
4. Looking back over the entire year of Medical Humanities I, how has your understanding of “My Why,” “My Learning Philosophy,” and “The Good Doctor” changed? Identify your most significant growth in empathy, professionalism, leadership, cultural humility, and social responsibility. What evidence supports this growth, and what concrete commitments will guide you in Level II?

## 4. Reflection and Evidence Rules

Reflection entries preserve the applicable source question, student response, subject and period/domain context, source-required milestone or plan, and evidence references.

Confirmed evidence requirements include:

- MED 101 milestone artifacts and the first- and second-period improvement-plan artifacts where the source says “Provide artifacts”.
- MED 102 domain-specific evidence listed in the domain inventory.
- MED 103 artifacts/evidence explicitly requested by the relevant questions, including the Student Social Responsibility Project artifact in Period 4.
- PLO evidence, artifact description/significance, and short reflection.

Suggested metadata such as title, date, related reflection, folder/domain, and related PLO supports organization but is not additional academic content. No patient names, faces, identifying dates, record numbers, or other patient-identifiable information may be required or uploaded.

## 5. Program Learning Outcomes

The global PLO section contains all eleven outcomes:

1. Demonstrate clinical competence.
2. Communicate effectively.
3. Lead and manage health care teams.
4. Engage in research activities.
5. Collaborate within interprofessional teams.
6. Utilize systems-based approach to healthcare.
7. Engage in continuing personal and professional development.
8. Adhere to ethical, professional, and legal standards.
9. Demonstrate nationalism, internationalism and dedication to service.
10. Practice the principles of social accountability.
11. Apply the core values of BukSU-COM to the medical practice in the communities.

Each PLO supports relevant student-selected evidence, an artifact description, and a short reflection. One artifact may support multiple PLOs with separate relevance explanations. The blueprint does not impose fixed subject-to-PLO mappings or automatically declare attainment.

## 6. Global Portfolio Sections and Level 1 Placement

### Home Page

The global Home Page contains All About Me, a three-quarter portrait photograph, a brief personal introduction, My Learning Philosophy, My Why, and The Good Doctor.

### Program Learning Outcomes

The eleven PLO documentation areas are a separate section under Level 1 and are not subject folders.

### Final Musings – Level 1

Final Musings remains a separate section under Level 1 with these five questions:

1. Has your learning philosophy changed?
2. Has your reason to pursue medicine changed?
3. What made them change? What made them remain as is?
4. How do you see your entire Level 1 journey contribute to your goal as a good doctor?
5. What was the single most important thing you learned in Level 1?

## 7. Integrity, Privacy, and Academic Presentation

Evidence must represent the student's work, experience, or contribution; explain what it demonstrates; use appropriate academic presentation; obtain required permissions; and acknowledge references, collaborators, and artificial-intelligence use. Patient confidentiality and private portfolio access remain mandatory.

## 8. Future-Level Placement

Future academic levels must remain configurable according to their eventual academic requirements. Their sections, subjects, folders, periods, domains, reflections, and evidence do not have to use the Level 1 placement.

## 9. Blueprint Limitations

The following are intentionally not finalized:

- Permanent final names of the twelve folders; current names are temporary configurable display values.
- MED 102 domain-to-folder grouping; current assignments are proposed only.
- Whether period themes are final folder names or nested learning periods.
- Any additional artifact file restrictions or limits.
