# Reflection and Artifact Mapping

Phase 2 – Stage 3

This document defines the internal academic content pattern for the proposed subject folders. It preserves source questions and does not create database tables, UI designs, or final MED 102 folder assignments.

## Status Rules

- **Confirmed by college document:** academic question, evidence instruction, or content explicitly present in the source.
- **Confirmed by client:** subject/folder count or project rule explicitly confirmed by the client.
- **Provisional:** an organizational alignment using source periods and provisional folder slots.
- **Pending clarification:** a folder/domain relationship or metadata rule not approved in the supplied materials.
- **Suggested metadata:** useful organization proposed for implementation, not an academic requirement.

## 1. Common Reflection Entry Pattern

Across all subjects, a reflection entry should preserve:

1. The applicable source question or questions.
2. The student’s response.
3. Subject and academic-period/domain context.
4. Any source-required milestone, plan, evidence, or artifact reference.
5. A related PLO association where the student identifies one.

The first four items are required by the applicable academic content. PLO association is a suggested organization unless the student identifies relevant evidence; no fixed subject-to-PLO mapping is imposed.

## 3. MED 102 – Family, Community, & Preventive Medicine I

MED 102 has four stable main folder IDs with proposed display groupings. The grouping is not client-approved. Each domain below remains a standalone nested learning-domain profile inside its proposed parent folder.

### Shared prescribed reflection questions

Each of the eight domains uses these exact questions:

1. **What did I learn?** Identify the most important insight from the activity.
2. **What changed in my understanding?** Explain how the experience challenged or strengthened your previous beliefs about health, illness, or healthcare delivery.
3. **Why does this matter to the people and communities we serve?** Relate your learning to patient, family, or community needs, preferably in the context of Bukidnon.
4. **How will I apply this as a future physician?** Identify a specific action or change in your future approach.

These questions are confirmed by the college document for every MED 102 domain.

## 4. MED 103 – Medical Humanities I

MED 103 has four period-to-folder alignments using stable IDs and temporary display values. Permanent names remain configurable.

### 4.1 MED103-F01 – Learning Identity & Humanistic Foundations

**Prescribed questions**

1. How have the principles of andragogy, learning, and memory influenced the way you study and take responsibility for your learning as a medical student? Reflect on one study practice you changed or need to change, and explain how this relates to your “My Learning Philosophy” and “My Why.”
2. Which historical development or medical tradition—from early medicine through Philippine medicine—most challenged or enriched your understanding of healing and the role of the physician? How did it influence your respect for scientific evidence, patients’ beliefs, and the social context of care?
3. As you were introduced to the patient–doctor relationship, what personal assumption about being a “good doctor” did you begin to question? Using a class discussion, scenario, or observed interaction, explain how empathy, respect, trust, and professional responsibility may influence a patient’s experience.
4. What personal milestone and what difficulty best describe your development during this bimonthly? Identify an artifact that demonstrates your learning and formulate one specific, realistic action to improve your self-directed learning, reflection, or humanistic attitudes in the next bimonthly.

**Expected reflection content:** Study responsibility, learning and memory, historical perspectives, the patient–doctor relationship, empathy, respect, trust, and professional responsibility.  
**Milestone:** Personal milestone and difficulty in the period.  
**Artifact:** Artifact demonstrating learning is explicitly requested in Question 4.  
**Improvement plan:** One specific, realistic action for the next bimonthly is explicitly requested in Question 4.  
**Relevant activities:** Class discussion, scenario, observed interaction, and study-practice reflection as named in the questions.

### 4.2 MED103-F02 – Patient Relationships & Cultural Understanding

**Prescribed questions**

1. Recall a patient narrative, role-play, or learning scenario in which a patient’s feelings, beliefs, circumstances, or expectations differed from the physician’s assumptions. How did listening, empathy, nonjudgmental communication, and trust-building change your interpretation of the patient’s behavior? What would you say or do differently now?
2. When a patient has difficulty understanding, accepting, or carrying out a proposed care plan, how can a physician advocate for and empower the patient without undermining autonomy? Reflect on an example and explain how patient feedback could be used to improve communication, quality of care, or the patient’s experience.
3. How have the discussions on cultural integration, diversity, and planetary/environmental health changed the way you understand illness and access to care? Consider local Bukidnon communities, including Indigenous Peoples where relevant, and examine how culture, living conditions, environment, or personal bias may affect respectful and equitable clinical decisions.
4. What learning activity or artifact most clearly demonstrates your progress in patient-centered communication, cultural humility, or advocacy? Describe what was challenging, the feedback or realization that mattered most, and a specific plan for practicing these competencies more consistently.

**Expected reflection content:** Listening, empathy, nonjudgmental communication, trust, advocacy, empowerment, autonomy, cultural integration, diversity, planetary/environmental health, Bukidnon communities, and cultural humility.  
**Milestone:** Progress in patient-centered communication, cultural humility, or advocacy.  
**Artifact:** Question 4 identifies a learning activity or artifact; no additional artifact format is imposed.  
**Improvement plan:** Specific plan for practicing competencies more consistently.  
**Relevant activities:** Patient narrative, role-play, learning scenario, discussions, and patient feedback as named in the questions.

### 4.3 MED103-F03 – Professional Identity, Ethics & Well-Being

**Prescribed questions**

1. How has your understanding of medical professionalism changed from viewing it as proper conduct to seeing it as a continuing commitment to patients, colleagues, and society? Reflect on a concrete situation in which your attitudes, integrity, respect, or sense of responsibility were tested.
2. Reflect on a simulated or observed professional dilemma involving compassion, honesty, confidentiality, accountability, a professional lapse, or peer behavior. Which ethical or professional principles—including those discussed in relation to the Hippocratic Oath and PMA/PRC standards—guided your judgment? What would you do, and why?
3. What did patient history-taking experiences or exercises in Pediatrics, OB-GYN, Internal Medicine, or Surgery teach you about adapting communication to different patients and working respectfully with health care teams? Identify a communication challenge, possible bias, or cultural consideration and explain how you would respond more effectively.
4. What evidence best demonstrates your growth in professionalism, teamwork, and self-awareness during this bimonthly? Explain how you will respond to feedback, address an identified weakness, and practice healthy self-care while maintaining professional responsibilities.

**Expected reflection content:** Professionalism, integrity, respect, responsibility, ethical principles, confidentiality, accountability, communication, teamwork, bias, cultural considerations, feedback, weakness, and self-care.  
**Milestone:** Growth in professionalism, teamwork, and self-awareness.  
**Artifact/evidence:** Question 4 requires evidence of growth; no specific artifact format is imposed.  
**Improvement plan:** Response to feedback, identified weakness, and healthy self-care.  
**Relevant activities:** Simulated or observed dilemmas, patient history-taking, and exercises in the listed disciplines.

### 4.4 MED103-F04 – Ethical Leadership & Social Responsibility

**Prescribed questions**

1. What did your experiences in group work, leadership activities, or management discussions reveal about your strengths and limitations as a future member or leader of a health care team? How would you use ethical leadership, collaboration, and quality-improvement principles to promote patient safety and resolve a team challenge?
2. Reflect on a disaster-risk-reduction activity, simulation, or scenario relevant to a Philippine community. What did it teach you about preparedness, coordinated response, risk communication, and recovery? Explain how you would balance immediate patient needs, limited resources, and care for vulnerable community members.
3. How did your Student Social Responsibility Project or other community-oriented learning experience deepen your understanding of service, social accountability, and the core values of BukSU-COM? Identify one result or limitation, whose perspective you may have overlooked, and a specific improvement you would propose. (Provide an artifact.)
4. Looking back over the entire year of Medical Humanities I, how has your understanding of “My Why,” “My Learning Philosophy,” and “The Good Doctor” changed? Identify your most significant growth in empathy, professionalism, leadership, cultural humility, and social responsibility. What evidence supports this growth, and what concrete commitments will guide you in Level II?

**Expected reflection content:** Ethical leadership, collaboration, quality improvement, patient safety, disaster preparedness, coordinated response, risk communication, recovery, vulnerable communities, service, social accountability, BukSU-COM values, and year-end integration.  
**Milestone:** Significant growth in empathy, professionalism, leadership, cultural humility, and social responsibility.  
**Required artifact:** Question 3 explicitly requires an artifact for the Student Social Responsibility Project or another community-oriented learning experience.  
**Improvement/forward plan:** Concrete commitments for Level II are explicitly requested in Question 4.  
**Relevant activities:** Group work, leadership activities, management discussions, disaster-risk-reduction activity/simulation/scenario, and the Student Social Responsibility Project.

## 5. Standard Artifact Information

### Required academic information

| Information | Requirement status | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Artifact title | Suggested metadata; required when the student submits an artifact record | Identifies the evidence |
| Description/significance | Confirmed by college document | Student explains why the artifact was selected and what it demonstrates |
| Supporting file or image | Required when a source question says “Provide artifacts” or identifies an artifact/evidence | Supplies the supporting evidence |
| Subject and academic context | Required organization for a subject artifact | Places evidence in the correct subject, period, or domain |

### Suggested metadata

| Information | Status | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Folder or learning domain | Suggested metadata; pending MED 102 mapping | Use a provisional domain reference until mapping is approved |
| Date or academic period | Suggested metadata | Use the academic period where applicable; exact date is not a universal source requirement |
| Related reflection | Suggested metadata | Links evidence to the response it supports |
| Related PLO | Suggested metadata | Student-selected association; do not impose fixed subject-to-PLO mappings |
| File type and size | Pending clarification | Client has not approved limits |

Artifact records must not require patient names, faces, identifying dates, record numbers, or other sensitive patient information. Clinical/community material must follow confidentiality and permission requirements.

## 6. Reusable Content Patterns

### Consistent across subjects

- Reflection prompt and student response.
- Academic context: subject plus period or domain.
- Artifact title and significance/description.
- Supporting file or image when required.
- Milestone or improvement-plan label where the source asks for one.
- Optional student-selected PLO associations.
- Confidentiality and academic-integrity guidance.

### Subject-specific

- MED 101 CERAE questions and milestone/improvement-artifact rules.
- MED 102 domain title, domain-specific evidence, and recurring four-question guide.
- MED 103 period theme, question-derived activities, evidence, and Level II commitments.

Consistency should organize the content without changing the source questions or adding requirements.
